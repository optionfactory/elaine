Tier × exposure matrix

Live repositories classified by operational criticality (tier) and network exposure (most permissive ingress across their environments). The top-left corner, high tier on the open internet, is where a vulnerability has the highest chance of turning into an incident, so it deserves attention first. Cell intensity reflects the combined tier × exposure weight. Select a cell to list its repositories in the ranking below; repositories without a manifest count as tier4 with unknown exposure.

Highest risk repositories

Ranked by tier × exposure score, then by known vulnerabilities and outdated dependencies as tie-breakers. Image vulns are the fixable high and critical vulnerabilities of the container images the repository runs: shown next to the ranking, not part of it.

Scanner failures

Repositories where a scanner could not complete (e.g. OSV unreachable, npm/cargo/mvn/go not available or failing, unparseable lockfiles). Their vulnerability and outdated-dependency counts are unknown, not zero: treat these numbers as missing until the check succeeds again.

Known vulnerabilities

Live repositories with known vulnerabilities, sorted by count. The same advisory often hits many repositories at once, so fixing a shared dependency once can clear multiple rows.

Container images

The images live repositories run, from their pinch configurations and Ansible playbooks, the most serious first. Each image is scanned once from the SBOM its registry publishes (it is not pulled), and only the vulnerabilities that already have a fix are listed: a newer image, or moving the repositories to its newer tag, removes them. They are kept apart from the vulnerabilities above, which are the dependencies' own.

Outdated dependencies

Live repositories by number of outdated dependencies, plus the artifacts that are stale across the organization: an artifact lagging in many repositories is usually worth a coordinated upgrade.

Artifacts stale across the organization

The same dependency aggregated across all live repositories, sorted by how many repositories it lags in. Entries near the top affect many projects at once and are usually worth a coordinated upgrade; multiple versions in the Latest column mean the repositories are pinned to different majors.
